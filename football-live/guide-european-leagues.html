A Guide to Europe's Top Football Leagues

Five domestic leagues dominate European club football by audience and money: England's Premier League, Spain's La Liga, Italy's Serie A, Germany's Bundesliga and France's Ligue 1. They share the same basic idea, a season-long table in which every club plays every other club home and away, but the details differ.

Size and format

The Premier League, La Liga and Serie A each have 20 clubs, so a season is 38 matches per team. The Bundesliga and Ligue 1 have 18 clubs, which means 34 matches per team. In all five, a win earns three points, a draw one and a defeat none. The club with the most points at the end is champion.

Promotion and relegation

The bottom clubs in each top division drop to the second tier and are replaced by the best clubs from below. The numbers vary by country, and some leagues add play-off matches for one of the places, so it is worth checking each league's current rules at the start of a season.

When the season runs

Most European leagues start in August and finish in May, with a winter break whose length depends on the country. Matches are usually played on weekends, with some games moved to weekday evenings to fit broadcasting schedules and European competitions.

Qualifying for Europe

The highest-placed clubs in each league qualify for UEFA competitions the following season: the Champions League for the top places, then the Europa League and the Conference League. Since the 2024-25 season the Champions League uses a single league phase of 36 clubs followed by knockout rounds, replacing the old group stage. Exact qualification spots per country depend on UEFA's rankings, so they can change from year to year.
