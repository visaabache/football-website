How to Watch Football Legally in Europe

Live football is sold through broadcasting rights, and those rights are sold country by country. A match shown by one broadcaster at home can be on a completely different service in another country. That is why searching for "where to watch" often gives confusing answers.

Start with the competition's official page

Leagues and UEFA publish "where to watch" pages that list the licensed broadcasters in each country. This is the most reliable starting point because it is maintained by the rights holder. Open it, choose your country, and you will see which television channel or streaming service carries your match.

Free-to-air and subscription services

In many countries a limited number of matches are shown on free channels, while most are behind a subscription. Some broadcasters offer day passes or monthly plans without a long contract, which can suit fans who only follow one team. Check the cancellation terms before you subscribe.

Highlights and scores without a subscription

Even without a subscription you can follow a match through live scores, official club and league highlights, and the radio commentary many public broadcasters provide online. This site lists fixtures, results and live scores so you know what is on and when.

Why we do not link to unlicensed streams

Unlicensed streams infringe copyright, often carry aggressive advertising and malware, and can disappear mid-match. We only link to official sources. If a site offers every match for free with no explanation of who holds the rights, treat that as a warning sign.

Practical tips

Check the kick-off time in your own time zone, since fixtures are usually announced in local time at the stadium. Look for the broadcaster a day ahead so there is time to sign in, and test your connection before big matches.
